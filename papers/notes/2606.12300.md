# Natural-Language Temporal Grounding in Hour-Long Videos is a Search Problem
arXiv 2606.12300v1 (10 Jun 2026) · NAVER Cloud AI + KAIST
Benchmark: ExtremeWhenBench · https://github.com/naver-ai/ExtremeWhenBench

**This is the paper that justifies our architecture on the slide. It is short, recent, and
every number in it argues for retrieve-then-ground. Quote it.**

## Core contribution
Not a system — an *empirical decomposition*. Claim: at hour scale, temporal grounding is
bottlenecked by **search** (finding the right window), not **recognition** (placing the
boundaries once you're near). They build the benchmark that can distinguish the two, then
show the split empirically.

ExtremeWhenBench: 2,273 queries / 194 videos, mean **75.7 min**, max 9 hr, median GT event
**9 s** — deliberately matched to Charades-STA's 7.1 s event grain, so the *only* thing that
changes is search-space size (153× larger).

## The four findings, with numbers

**Table 1 — hour-scale collapse (mIoU, Charades-STA → ExtremeWhenBench):**
| Model | Charades | Hour-scale | Drop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.5-9B | 0.579 | 0.110 | 5.3× |
| InternVL3.5-8B | 0.359 | 0.003 | 120× |
| LLaVA-OneVision-7B | 0.226 | 0.003 | 75× |
| GPT-5.4 (64f) | 0.299 | 0.013 | 23× |
| Gemini-3.5-flash (auto-fps) | 0.466 | 0.115 | 4.1× |
| **CLIP ViT-L/14-336 @1fps** | 0.332 | **0.269** | **1.2×** |

**A plain CLIP frame-retrieval baseline beats every open VideoLLM and every closed VideoLLM
at hour scale.** No grounding training, no LLM in the loop. On short video it was mid-pack.

**Finding 3 — the cross-over.** Crop to a GT-centered window of varying width (varies search
difficulty, holds localization difficulty fixed). At a 30 s window both VideoLLMs hit their
Charades ceiling (Qwen 0.572, Gemini-3.5 0.578 vs 0.579 native) — **localization is intact,
it's search that breaks.** Qwen crosses below CLIP around a ~20 min window; Gemini-3.5-flash
only crosses at full 76 min. The cross-over point scales with the model's long-context capacity.

**Finding 4 — the hybrid (Table 2), the money result:**
| Pipeline | VideoLLM context | mIoU |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.5-9B monolithic, N=768 | 76 min | 0.053 |
| CLIP retrieval + rule-based thresholding | 76 min | 0.269 |
| **CLIP retrieval → Qwen3.5-9B grounding** | **6 min** | **0.354** |

**6.7× over monolithic, 1.32× over retrieval-alone, using 12× less VideoLLM context.**
Hybrid config: CLIP **top-K=3**, each expanded **±1 min**, **NMS at 60 s**.
**Peaks at K=3 and degrades at K=10** — because at K=10 the VideoLLM is back to searching
20 min of context. *The reranker's job is to keep k small, not to be generous.*

**Failure taxonomy** (100 random IoU<0.05 cases, Qwen N=2048; 1,817/2,273 fail this threshold):
**85% search_fail, 11% localization_fail, 4% uncertain, 0% parse, 0% refusal.**

**Frame-count sweep (Fig 3):** on Charades, mIoU peaks at N=64 and is flat after — more frames
carry no new information. On hour-scale it rises monotonically and is *still climbing at
N=2,048*. But even at N=2,048 (0.110) it trails retrieval-alone (0.269). **Scaling frames
does not close the gap.** This is the empirical death of "just use a bigger context window".

## Stated limitations / honesty notes
- The retrieval stage is a bare CLIP baseline; they explicitly name "replace retrieval with a
  stronger temporally-aware retriever and see if the hybrid ceiling rises" as future work.
  **That open question is exactly the space our project sits in.**
- Content is lecture/documentary/general web video (sourced from LVBench, MLVU, VideoMME).
  No surveillance, no aerial, no static-camera footage. Our domain shift is untested here.
- Closed models were run "under our compute budget" — Gemini/GPT numbers are budget-limited,
  not ceilings.
- mIoU only in the main text; R@τ and parse-failure rates are in the appendix.

## What we steal for a 7-hour hackathon on CCTV/drone footage

1. **The framing sentence for our demo.** "85% of hour-scale grounding failures are search
   failures, not recognition failures — so we spent our compute on the index, not the VLM."
   That is the whole pitch, and it is citable to a June 2026 paper.

2. **Hard baseline numbers to beat.** Their hybrid = retrieval top-3, ±1 min, NMS 60 s, then
   VLM. **Implement exactly this as our v0 baseline in `search.py` on day one.** It is maybe
   30 lines, it is the published SOTA-ish recipe, and everything we add gets measured as a
   delta over it. This is our `runs.jsonl` row #1.

3. **K=3, not K=10.** Start the retrieval budget at top-3 windows expanded ±1 min with 60 s
   NMS. If we're tempted to raise k, the paper says accuracy *drops*. Make k a swept parameter
   in `evaluate.py` and reproduce their K curve on our footage — that is a free, credible
   measurable-performance plot.

4. **The GT-centered window sweep is a ~20-line experiment with a great payoff.** Crop our
   footage to 30 s / 1 m / 5 m / 20 m / full around each ground-truth event and plot Qwen3-VL
   mIoU vs window length. It (a) proves our VLM's localizer is fine, (b) locates *our* system's
   search/localize cross-over, (c) tells us how wide our retrieved windows should be. Nobody
   else in that room will have this plot.

5. **Report mIoU alongside T*'s temporal F1.** Two metrics from two papers, both standard,
   costs nothing extra since we already have GT intervals.

6. **⚠️ Direct warning for Task 4 (tonight's dry run): DO NOT pad a short clip with unrelated
   footage to fake a long video.** They tested exactly this: Qwen drops 0.572 → 0.247 (10 min
   padded) → 0.183 (20 min padded), and **47%/74.5% of the worst failures land inside the
   padding** — because a generic query like "a person walks through a doorway" matches the
   padding just as well as the true segment. Their fix is our fix: **use naturally long
   footage, and verify each (query, interval) pair is unique within its video.** For CCTV this
   matters even more — a parking lot at 14:00 looks like the same parking lot at 15:00.
   **Our eval queries must be written to be uniquely satisfiable, and we should check
   uniqueness explicitly.** Cheap check: run the query through our own retriever and eyeball
   whether the top-10 are all near the GT.

7. **Their query-diversity discipline, adapted.** They reject template-bound queries (TVBench:
   five 4-gram prefixes cover 99.9% of a split) and measure MATTR. Our 20 hand-written eval
   queries should not all start "when does a person…". Deliberately spread across: object
   presence, attribute, count, OCR/text, motion/behaviour, absence/negation, multi-camera.
   Then **report per-category recall** — the per-category breakdown is where our "T* fails on
   behaviour queries" thesis either shows up as a number or doesn't.

8. **Their benchmark-construction pipeline is a labelling shortcut if we're short on time.**
   1 fps captions → LLM groups them into events → verify boundaries on an **8×8 frame mosaic**
   → dedup → generate one 8–18 word question per event. That is a semi-automatic way to
   produce eval queries with GT timestamps from unlabelled footage in minutes.
   Note the 8×8 mosaic for boundary verification is **the same tiling trick as T\*** — two
   independent papers using grid-tiling to make a VLM look at many frames cheaply.
   Caveat: 3.3% of their auto-mined events were rejected and 20.6% had boundaries refined by
   the verifier, so **auto-generated GT needs a human pass** — budget 20 min for eyeballing.
