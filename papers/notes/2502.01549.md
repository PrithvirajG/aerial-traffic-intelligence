# VideoRAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Extreme Long-Context Videos
arXiv 2502.01549v1 (Feb 2025) · HKU + Baidu · Code: https://github.com/HKUDS/VideoRAG
⚠️ Name collision: this is a *different* paper from Video-RAG (2411.13093). Cite carefully.

## Core contribution
A **dual-channel index** for a corpus of many long videos (not one video):
1. **Graph channel** — captions + ASR are turned into a cross-video *knowledge graph*
   (entities + relations), so retrieval can hop across videos and connect entities that
   never co-occur in a single clip.
2. **Embedding channel** — clip-level multi-modal embeddings (ImageBind) preserve visual
   nuance that captioning throws away (lighting, texture, fine object detail).
Retrieval hits both channels, unions the results, then an LLM filters.
Benchmark contribution: **LongerVideos**, 164 videos / 134.6 h / 602 queries across
lecture, documentary, entertainment.

## Exact method

**Indexing**
- Segment every video into **30-second clips** (fixed, not scene-based).
- Per clip: sample **k = 5** frames chronologically → VLM caption `C_j`, conditioned on the
  clip's ASR transcript `T_j` (transcript is fed *into* the captioning prompt — cheap way to
  make captions query-relevant and less generic).
- ASR per clip via Distil-Whisper → `T_j`.
- Merge `(C_j, T_j)` per clip, concatenate clips into length-bounded **text chunks**
  (a chunk spans several clips).
- LLM extracts entities + relations per chunk → incremental knowledge graph over all videos,
  with (i) entity unification/merging across videos, (ii) dynamic graph growth as new videos
  arrive, (iii) LLM-synthesized unified entity descriptions.
- Separately: each 30 s clip → ImageBind embedding. Index = `(G, E_chunks, E_clips)`.

**Retrieval (two paths + a filter)**
- *Textual path*: LLM reformulates query into a declarative sentence → match against entity
  descriptions in the graph → GraphRAG-style chunk ranking → chunks → the clips they cover.
- *Visual path*: LLM rewrites the query into a **scene description** before embedding.
  Their example: "what color is the car that chases the main character through the city?" →
  "An intense urban chase sequence featuring a car pursuing someone through city streets,
  with buildings and traffic in the background." Then cosine sim vs clip embeddings, top-K.
- *LLM-Judge filter*: binary relevance call on each retrieved clip, drops noise.

**Answering (two-stage / re-caption on demand)**
- LLM extracts keywords `K_q` from the query.
- **Re-caption the surviving clips at higher resolution**: k̂ = 15 frames (vs 5 at index time),
  prompted with `K_q`. So indexing is cheap and generic; answering is expensive and
  query-specific, on ~10 clips instead of thousands. **This is the key efficiency idea.**
- Feed query-specific captions + retrieved text chunks to the LLM.

## Headline numbers (and their conditions)
- Win-rate vs NaiveRAG: 53.3% overall; vs GraphRAG-local 53.9%; vs GraphRAG-global ~55.8%;
  vs LightRAG ~57.1%. **These are 50–58% win rates — modest, not dominant.**
- 5-point LLM-judge score vs NaiveRAG baseline, "all" column:
  VideoAgent 1.98 · LLaMA-VID 2.44 · NotebookLM 3.37 · **VideoRAG 4.45**.
- Ablation: −Graph and −Vision both drop win rate; reported only as a bar figure, **no table
  of numbers**.
- Stack: quantized MiniCPM-V (VLM), Distil-Whisper (ASR), ImageBind (multimodal encoder),
  OpenAI text-embedding-3-small, **GPT-4o-mini as the core LLM for indexing, retrieval and
  generation**.

## Stated / evident limitations
- **Evaluation is entirely LLM-as-judge win-rate.** No Recall@k, no temporal IoU, no latency
  table, no ground-truth timestamps. There is no way to tell from this paper whether
  retrieval or generation is responsible for the gains.
- **Not actually local.** Despite the open-source framing, GPT-4o-mini + OpenAI embeddings are
  in the critical path. The "runs on one 3090" claim in our CONTEXT.md is from the repo
  README, not from this paper — the paper's baselines used 48 GB GPUs.
- Fixed 30 s clips, uniform k=5 sampling. No motion prefilter, no shot detection.
- Knowledge-graph construction is LLM-call-per-chunk — the dominant indexing cost, and it
  scales with *duration*, not with *information content*.
- Content is lecture/documentary/entertainment: **speech-heavy**. The graph is largely built
  out of ASR entities.

## What we steal for a 7-hour hackathon on CCTV/drone footage
1. **Two-stage captioning — this is the single best idea in the paper.**
   Index cheap (k=5 frames, generic caption), then **re-caption only the top-k retrieved
   chunks at k̂=15 frames with the query in the prompt.** Directly applicable: our
   `enrich.py` writes a cheap generic caption; `answer.py` re-reads the actual frames of the
   ~10 surviving chunks with a query-conditioned prompt. Makes the index cheap enough to
   cover hours of footage while keeping answer quality high, and it's an easy measurable
   ablation (index-time cost vs answer accuracy).
2. **Feed the transcript into the captioning prompt.** Our analogue with no audio: feed the
   *OCR text and detector output* of the chunk into the caption prompt. Captions stop being
   "a parking lot with cars" and start being "a white van at gate B, timestamp 14:32".
3. **Scene-description query rewriting before embedding.** Surveillance queries are terse and
   in the wrong register for SigLIP2 ("did anyone tailgate through the door?"). Rewriting to
   "two people passing through a doorway closely one after another, security camera view"
   should measurably lift dense recall. **Cheap A/B on our eval set — one prompt, one number.**
4. **LLM-Judge relevance filter as our reranker.** Qwen3-VL-4B, binary yes/no per candidate
   chunk. Slots straight into `search.py` as the rerank stage.
5. **Cross-video / cross-camera entity linking is the FlytBase-shaped idea.** Their KG merges
   entities across videos; our version merges *the same vehicle/person across cameras and
   time*. "This white van appears at Cam 3 14:02, Cam 1 14:09, Cam 4 14:31" is a genuinely
   useful security answer and is exactly the multi-camera setting FlytBase operates in.
   **But it is the most expensive thing in the paper — do it only if the core pipeline is
   green by 3 PM.** A degraded version (link chunks by shared OCR plate text or by appearance
   embedding similarity) gets 80% of the demo value for 10% of the work.

## What we deliberately do NOT take
- **The full LLM knowledge graph.** One LLM call per chunk over hours of CCTV is exactly the
  cost profile our motion prefilter exists to avoid, and it buys ~4 percentage points of
  win-rate in *their* speech-heavy setting where the graph is built from transcripts we
  won't have. Skip it.
- **Their evaluation methodology.** LLM-judge win-rate is unfalsifiable in a demo room. Our
  `evaluate.py` with ground-truth timestamps and Recall@k is strictly stronger, and the
  contrast is worth one sentence on the final slide: *"the SOTA long-video RAG paper reports
  no retrieval metrics; here are ours."*
